01Equity Time-Series Forecasting

statsmodels · ARMA/ARIMA · fixed-origin holdout · 252 values verified per run

Negative result

Training-only BIC selects MA(2). It beats a zero-return forecast by 0.29% of RMSE on an untouched 20-day holdout — while Ljung–Box rejects residual adequacy at lag 20 and ARCH-LM rejects constant variance (p = 4e-09).

Fit, residual adequacy and out-of-sample performance are kept as three separate questions, because a model can win the first and fail the other two. Here it does. Selection is specified before the holdout is touched, benchmarks are fixed in advance, and return and log-price errors are never compared on the same scale.

02Shanghai–Hong Kong Market Linkages

VAR / VECM · Johansen · Granger · 334 values verified per run

Negative result

A Shanghai shock appears to explain 32.4% of Hong Kong's 10-step forecast-error variance — reverse the Cholesky ordering and the same quantity becomes 0.35%. Same data, same model, same sample; only the identifying assumption changed.

VAR(7) also loses to a zero-return benchmark on MAE in both markets, and neither Granger direction is significant at 5%. Johansen selects rank 2 stably, yet every fitted VECM rejects residual whiteness — so no long-run equilibrium claim is made and no VECM forecast is deployed on the back of it.

03China's New Energy Vehicle Transition, 2015–2025

pandas · matplotlib · 7 compiled tables · 16 figures, bilingual

NEV share of new vehicle sales went from 1.3% to 47.9% in eleven years. The findings that complicate that story: charging capacity outgrew the fleet but almost entirely through private chargers (public share fell 48% → 23%); battery chemistry reversed toward LFP (40% → 81%); and plug-in hybrids gained share (17% → 36%).

Every denominator, definition change and scenario assumption is documented — including a 0.3 GWh discrepancy against an official battery total, and a carbon estimate captioned on the figure itself as a scenario rather than a statistic.

04Bike-Sharing Demand: Interactive Visualisation

Plotly · Pyecharts · 17,379 hourly observations

Data-quality finding

The two files in the UCI Bike Sharing Dataset store dates as 2011/1/1 and 2011-01-01, so the obvious join between them matches 0 of 731 days — and any reconciliation check built on it passes while comparing nothing to nothing. Parsed properly, the files agree exactly.

Four interactive figures, each labelling what every visual channel encodes. The density contour is a count of observations; ride volume is carried only by marker size on a separate layer, so the two can never be read off the same channel.

05Beijing PM2.5, 2010–2014

Seaborn · 43,824 hourly observations · 7 figures

Method correction

PM2.5 exceeded China's grade II daily limit in 48.5% of hours. What explains the variation is ventilation, not temperature: Spearman ρ = −0.360 for wind speed against +0.011 for temperature, and median PM2.5 is 6.3× higher in the calmest wind decile than the windiest.

Temperature's Pearson r (−0.091) and Spearman ρ (+0.011) disagree on the sign, so the linear coefficient my coursework version led with was describing an association that is not monotonic. Separately, 1,341 of the 2,067 missing hours sit inside gaps longer than a day — the longest is 155 consecutive hours — so 91% of them are left missing rather than interpolated into a smooth ramp through a possible haze episode.

06Consumer Transactions: A Segmentation That Isn't There

Matplotlib · 8,000 transactions · bootstrap CIs · ANOVA · permutation null

Null result

The brief asks which customer groups spend more. Tested first: four of the five candidate segmentations do not differ at all — age band p = 0.45, gender 0.52, city tier 0.77, payment method 0.90. Income's correlation with spend is −0.010, 95% CI [−0.032, +0.012]: narrow enough to be a measured absence rather than a missing finding.

Only membership tier separates (p = 1.0 × 10⁻⁶, robust to 10% trimming), and its direction is unidentified because loyalty tiers are awarded for spending. Monthly revenue falls inside a permutation null band in all 12 months, so there is no seasonality to explain either.
