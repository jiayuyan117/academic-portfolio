Equity Time-Series Forecasting¶

ARMA / ARIMA Diagnostics and Benchmark Evaluation¶

Coursework-based quantitative portfolio · Python / statsmodels · Reproducible analysis

Research question and main finding¶

Can parsimonious linear time-series models improve on simple forecasts of the supplied Kweichow Moutai price series? This study distinguishes a model's fit, its residual adequacy, and its performance on an untouched forecast window.

Within a pre-specified candidate set, training-only BIC selects MA(2) (BIC 12274.6038). Its fixed-origin 20-observation return RMSE is 2.218777 percentage points, versus 2.225323 for a zero-return forecast: an RMSE improvement of 0.294% on this window. This is a descriptive comparison, not evidence of statistically significant or tradable outperformance.

Data and audit trail¶

GZMTp.csv is the unmodified course-supplied dataset: 2,911 prices from 2010-01-04 through 2021-12-31. The original notebook identifies the series as Kweichow Moutai. An independently verified vendor, adjustment policy, and complete dividend treatment were not supplied. Accordingly, the study describes the supplied closing-price series, not a total-return investment.

The date field is Time; close is the supplied positive closing price. Dates are sorted and checked for uniqueness, missing values and finite positive prices before log transformation. No synthetic weekends or holidays are inserted. The transformation is z = 100 ln(P); returns are first differences of z. A return value of 1 means a 1% log return; forecast errors are measured in percentage points.

There are 2,890 training returns, ending 2021-12-03. The final 20 observations, starting 2021-12-06, form one fixed-origin holdout. The first test return correctly uses the last training price as its lagged price. No random split or observed-test-value updating is used.

Methods¶

  1. Diagnose stationarity on the complete training interval with constant-only ADF regressions and AIC lag selection; inspect return ACF/PACF.
  2. Estimate AR(2), MA(2) and ARMA(2,1) on exactly the same training returns. Report parameters, AIC, BIC and convergence. BIC is specified before test evaluation.
  3. Refit the BIC-selected model independently. Inspect residual time plots and ACF, and use Ljung–Box at lags 10 and 20 with model_df = p + q.
  4. Forecast the final 20 returns in one call, with fixed parameters. Compare the model with zero-return and training-mean baselines on aligned dates.
  5. Fit ARIMA(1,1,1) to undifferenced z with and without drift. Compare with a last-value baseline on the same z scale, then transform forecasts to prices.
  6. Synthesize the evidence, add ARCH diagnostics, and inspect selection on earlier 60% and 80% training prefixes without exposing the final holdout to tuning.

The primary research notebook presents the complete numerical analysis in English. Original teaching material is retained separately for provenance.

Diagnostics and interpretation¶

For MA(2), residual Ljung–Box p-values are 0.163988 at lag 10 and 0.0275137 at lag 20. At the 5% level, the decisions are respectively do not reject and reject for the joint zero-autocorrelation null. Thus the BIC winner is a provisional comparison model, not a fully adequate final specification: the lag-20 check detects remaining linear dependence. The tests do not establish independence, normality or constant variance. The supplementary 10-lag ARCH-LM p-value is 4.03846e-09; the test rejects its no-ARCH null. Its usual asymptotic interpretation is a diagnostic, not a guarantee that a specific volatility model is correct.

The earlier-prefix BIC results are in results/training_selection_stability.csv. These describe sensitivity to the estimation period. BIC values from different sample sizes are not ranked against one another.

const in the stationary ARIMA parameterization is the long-run location of returns; the complete conditional mean also depends on lagged centered returns and innovations. An MA term represents lagged innovations, not an arithmetic rolling average of prices. For integrated models, trend="t" creates drift after differencing, while trend="n" omits deterministic drift.

Under a conditional Gaussian log-price model, exp(predicted z / 100) is a price median, not a mean. The mean additionally uses exp(forecast variance / 20000). Transforming the two prediction-interval endpoints preserves their ordering and produces an asymmetric price interval. These plug-in intervals do not fully account for parameter uncertainty.

Limitations and defensible next steps¶

One stock and one short holdout do not establish generalization. Market regimes, corporate actions, price adjustment conventions and volatility clustering can affect inference. The Gaussian, linear, constant-parameter models do not encode trading costs, execution, economic identification or investment utility. A small RMSE advantage is not automatically meaningful, and no strategy return is reported.

A stronger follow-up would pre-register expanding-window evaluation across several non-overlapping forecast origins, use training-only nested model selection, verify adjusted prices and dividends, and compare conditional-variance models and calibrated prediction intervals. These are proposed extensions, not claimed completed experiments.
