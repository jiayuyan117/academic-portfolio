Shanghai–Hong Kong Market Linkages¶

Research question and principal finding¶

Do lagged Shanghai and Hong Kong index returns provide useful incremental predictive information, and how sensitive are apparent market linkages to model specification and shock identification? This coursework-based study separates stationarity, prediction, Granger testing, and variance decomposition rather than treating them as interchangeable evidence of causality. VAR(7) marginally improves Shanghai holdout RMSE relative to zero returns but does not improve Hong Kong RMSE. Neither Granger direction is significant at 5%. The shock contribution is highly sensitive to Cholesky ordering, and the separate macro-financial cointegration exercise does not establish a robust long-run equilibrium.

The files are course supplied. Their upstream vendor, collection process, redistribution rights, adjustment conventions, and exact observation timestamps have not been independently verified. The labels identify Shanghai Composite (SZH) and Hang Seng (XGH) according to the starter. Dates are sorted and checked for uniqueness; values must be finite and index prices strictly positive. No missing trading dates are inserted and no interpolation is performed. The two datasets are not merged.

In VARSJ, returns are 100 × the first difference of natural-log prices. A value of 1 is a 1% log return; forecast errors are in percentage points. The final 20 common trading observations form a fixed-origin holdout. All return-model lag selection, estimation, ADF, Granger, IRF, and FEVD calculations use training data only. Forecasts are generated once from the final seven training observations, without refitting or feeding in realized holdout returns. The zero-return forecast is a prespecified benchmark, not a fitted model.

XZSJ is a separate in-sample teaching exercise. Its SZH is already logged and is not logged again. SHIBOR is the 7-day rate according to the course; EX is yuan per USD. The course labels do not independently verify a market-data vendor or daily fixing convention.

Core empirical results¶

  1. With constant-only ADF, maxlag=12, AIC autolag, training SZH log-price p = 0.4050725522, and return p = 1.061710611e-29. Log prices fail to reject a unit root, whereas returns reject it. This supports using returns in a stable VAR; it does not establish independent, Gaussian, or homoskedastic returns.
  2. VAR(7) predicts 2021-12-03 log returns of 0.10606119% for SZH and 0.03782405% for XGH. These are conditional means, not confidence bounds or price forecasts.
  3. The Johansen trace test with det_order=0, q=2, and 5% significance selects rank 2. The first nonrejection is row 3 (null r ≤ 2): trace 2.447203 versus critical value 3.841500. q counts lagged differences and corresponds to a level VAR order of q+1.
  4. The SZH → XGH system F test gives F = 1.27582348, p = 0.2579153178. The seven lagged SZH coefficients in the XGH equation are not jointly significant at 5%. Failure to reject is not proof of no linkage.
  5. In the explicit order [SZH, XGH], fevd(10).decomp[1, 9, 0] * 100 is 32.42815216%: the contribution of the identified SZH shock to XGH's ten-step forecast-error variance. This is neither a share of observed returns nor a structural causal effect.

For SZH shocks explaining XGH at ten steps, changing [SZH, XGH] to [XGH, SZH] changes the share from 32.428152% to 0.350971%. Reordering changes the contemporaneous zero restrictions and the definition of orthogonal shocks. It does not represent an economic change in the underlying sample. Responses and shocks are remapped by variable name when comparing orders. IRF bands in the research notebook are asymptotic pointwise 95% intervals, not simultaneous path bands.

Cointegration sensitivity and why the conclusion is limited¶

Constant-only ADF checks use maxlag=11 and AIC autolag for XZSJ:

All three level series fail to reject a unit root in this constant-only ADF specification; all first differences reject. This is consistent with an I(1) system, not proof of integration order, and the baseline tests do not establish mixed integration. If other justified specifications indicate a stationary component, that component can contribute to a positive system rank without establishing a long-run equilibrium among three I(1) variables. Unit-root tests have limited power and depend on deterministic terms and breaks.

The bivariate SZH/SHIBOR Engle–Granger test gives p = 0.61832824. Its variable set differs from the trivariate Johansen system, so their outcomes are not direct contradictions.

Johansen uses det_order=0 and the 5% trace rule; VECM estimates use an unrestricted outside constant (co), preserving the course demonstration. The two APIs' deterministic encodings are not interchangeable; broader deterministic-specification robustness has not been established. Rank remains 2 across q=1, 2, and 3, but all fitted VECMs reject residual whiteness. This limited rank stability does not resolve the residual misspecification. Consequently the cointegration output remains a conditional teaching result, not a robust investment, policy, or equilibrium claim. The analysis does not deploy a VECM forecast or select the return VAR using XZSJ.

Methods, provenance and reproducibility¶

This analysis develops supplied econometrics coursework and retains its original datasets and teaching notebook in source_materials. AI assistance was used for code completion, analytical organization, verification, and English writing. The data were supplied with the course; upstream vendor and price-adjustment details were not independently established.

The English research notebook is fully executed. The runner verifies source hashes, recomputes the analysis and compares numerical evidence with the established reference results before exporting self-contained HTML and a file manifest. Exact dependency versions and the Python environment are recorded.

Future work could use preregistered rolling-origin evaluation on additional data, robust inference for financial residuals, structural-break tests, and economically justified shock identification. These are proposed next steps, not completed analyses.
