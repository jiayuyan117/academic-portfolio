04 / Visual analytics · Jiayu Yan

Reconcile demand before visualizing it.

Can historical ride patterns help frame fleet-planning questions without confusing observation density with demand?

Data and quality check

The UCI Capital Bikeshare data contain 17,379 hourly observations and 731 daily rows from Washington D.C., 2011–2012. Raw date strings use different formats: a string join matches no days. Parsed dates reconcile hourly and daily totals with zero ride difference; unmatched dates now raise an error.

Findings and visual encodings

Registered riders account for 81.2% of recorded rides, not unique people. The busiest day is 15 September 2012 with 8,714 rides. Just three severe-weather hours preclude a reliable severe-weather recommendation.

  • Sunburst: area encodes ride totals; hover shows share of parent.
  • Density contour: contours count observed hours; marker size encodes rides per hour.
  • Calendar: inspect actual dates and switch years on a common colour scale.
  • Season animation: a seeded sample excludes the quietest decile; it is not the full distribution.

Each chart bundles its data and Plotly runtime for local viewing.

Decision and next evidence

Check evening availability and regular-user service reliability. Citywide totals cannot locate stockouts or explain why people ride. Join station inventories, failed rentals and operating costs before evaluating a scheduling pilot against a comparable baseline. No pilot or causal effect is claimed.
