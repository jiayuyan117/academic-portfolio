06 / Statistical inference · Jiayu Yan

Segment differences are not campaign effects.

Does amount per transaction justify prioritizing a demographic segment or membership offer?

Data and original evidence

Removing 20 exact duplicates leaves 8,000 transactions and 3,997 user IDs. Original collection and synthetic status are unknown. Four of five nominal ANOVA groupings do not reject their nulls; that does not establish equality. Amount is not margin, conversion or customer lifetime value.

Added user-cluster sensitivity

The existing analysis resamples all 3,997 user IDs with replacement for 2,000 draws (seed 20261013). Each selected ID contributes all its transactions, including across groups. The estimand remains the difference in transaction-weighted arithmetic means, not an equal-weighted customer mean.

For Standard minus Gold membership, the difference is −215.54 yuan per transaction, with pointwise 95% interval [−309.62, −119.73]. This is a descriptive association, not an upgrade effect. All observed pairs are exported, without selecting by result.

Inspect every pair, interval and valid-draw count. Intervals are exploratory, not simultaneous or multiplicity-adjusted; they supply no omnibus p-value.

Decision and limits

Do not reallocate marketing spend from these rankings alone. The bootstrap allows within-user dependence but assumes reliable IDs and independent, representative user clusters. It does not resolve confounding, selection or dependence between users.

Validate user identity and assignment rules, then consider a randomized offer experiment with incremental contribution margin as the primary outcome. No such experiment or commercial gain is claimed.
